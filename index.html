About Me

I'm a proud Guatemalan and an M.S. student in Transportation Engineering at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). I completed my B.S. in Industrial Engineering and Operations Research at UC Berkeley, where I built a strong foundation in optimization, simulation, data analysis, and algorithmic decision-making. My research interests center on optimization, machine learning for transportation, and routing problems, with a particular focus on scalable methods for shared mobility, first-/last-mile access, and network operations, where mathematical models must account for real demand patterns, behavioral constraints, and operational tradeoffs.

At UCLA, I am conducting research with the HUMAN Lab under the supervision of Prof. Youngseo Kim. I hope to pursue a Ph.D. focused on algorithmic transportation systems by developing methods that are technically rigorous, computationally practical, and useful for real mobility decisions. I care about producing reproducible work that helps make transportation easier, more accessible, and more effective.

I also enjoy teaching and mentoring. Through Calyber, Cal Transpo, and outreach initiatives, I have developed learning materials and helped students build confidence in machine learning, optimization, transportation analytics, and research methods. Outside of academics, I advocate for autism awareness, inspired by my younger brother. I also enjoy playing guitar and piano, following soccer, and supporting Borussia Dortmund. Heja BVB!
