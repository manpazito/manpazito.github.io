Drone Battery Consumption Modeling for Autonomous Medical Delivery Systems

For my UC Berkeley senior design project, I worked with a team to estimate how much battery charge an autonomous medical delivery flight would use. We focused on estimates that could be made before takeoff from characteristics of a planned route.

Modeling approach

We summarized individual flight records into one set of route features per flight. This let us compare two ways of estimating total charge use from the same inputs.

  • Linear regression provided a baseline for relating route characteristics to total charge use.
  • A tree-based model allowed for nonlinear relationships between those same inputs and charge use.

Evaluation and battery planning

We compared predictions with recorded charge use and examined underestimates specifically. An underestimate matters for planning because it leaves less charge available on arrival than expected. We explored how a planning reserve could account for that uncertainty.

What we found

In retrospective testing, the model underestimated charge use on some flights held out of training. That made underestimation a separate planning concern from overall prediction error. We evaluated the two separately because a model can have low average error while still underestimating the charge needed for an individual flight.

My current drone allocation research explores how battery reserves and other constraints shape task and routing decisions.
