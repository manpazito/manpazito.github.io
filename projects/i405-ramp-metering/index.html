I-405 Southbound On-Ramp Metering Case Study

At the Ventura Boulevard loop on-ramp to southbound I-405 in Sherman Oaks, how much can ramp metering ease freeway congestion, and how much delay shifts to the ramp?

Study setup

The microscopic simulation uses the ramp geometry and observed Caltrans PeMS traffic data from October 11, 2022. Across 1,400 runs, the study compares no metering, a fixed release rate, a Caltrans-style traffic-responsive plan with queue override, and ALINEA occupancy feedback with queue protection. It varies demand, downstream capacity, driver behavior, detector availability, and controller settings.

Simulation findings

At the observed demand, none of the metering strategies met the study’s threshold for a change in freeway congestion relative to no metering. With storage for about 25 vehicles, queue protection governed the two responsive controllers for roughly three-quarters of the simulated morning.

Under some lighter-demand scenarios, ALINEA reduced freeway congestion without a clear reduction in time per vehicle. The benefit to freeway traffic came with more waiting on the ramp.

The comparison uses one observed day, a partially calibrated model with one freeway lane, and assumed Caltrans controller settings because the ramp’s actual plan is unpublished. The findings describe modeled scenarios.
