Stochastic Modeling of Airline Cabin Boarding Efficiency

Header photo by Corbis via Getty Images, as published in Forbes.

A discrete-time, agent-based simulation framework for studying commercial aircraft boarding procedures. This project models passengers, aircraft geometry, overhead bins, and movement constraints to support controlled experiments in boarding efficiency.

Developed for INDENG 174: Simulation for Enterprise-Scale Systems, University of California, Berkeley (Fall 2025).

GitHub Repo

How the simulator works

Each passenger has an assigned seat, a walking pace, and a baggage stow time. As passengers enter and move along the aisle, they may have to wait for space to walk, stow a bag, or reach their seat. The simulator tracks when passengers enter and sit down, how congestion changes, and how long it takes to seat everyone.

What we found

We compared five strategies across repeated simulation runs. Random boarding had the lowest mean total boarding time at 452.2 ticks, followed by Steffen at 489.1, Wilma at 557.7, front-to-back at 562.5, and back-to-front at 572.7.

The study also varied baggage rates, stow times, walking speeds, and passenger no-shows. The reported times are simulation ticks and reflect the cabin layout and passenger behavior represented in the model.
